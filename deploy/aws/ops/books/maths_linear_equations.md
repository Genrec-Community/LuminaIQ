# Chapter 2 Linear Equations in One Variable

## 2.1 Introduction
In earlier classes you met algebraic expressions such as $x + 3$, $2y - 5$ and $3p + 7$. An equation is a statement that two expressions are equal, for example $2x + 3 = 11$. The expression on the left of the equals sign is called the left hand side (LHS) and the one on the right is the right hand side (RHS). An equation in which the highest power of the variable is 1 is called a linear equation. In this chapter we study linear equations in one variable, such as $5x - 4 = 21$ or $\frac{y}{3} + 2 = 7$.

## 2.2 Solving Equations with the Variable on One Side
The value of the variable that makes LHS equal to RHS is called the solution of the equation. We can add, subtract, multiply or divide both sides of an equation by the same number (not zero for division) and the equation stays balanced, like a weighing balance with equal weights on both pans.

Example 1: Solve $2x - 3 = 7$.
Add 3 to both sides: $2x = 10$. Divide both sides by 2: $x = 5$.
Check: LHS $= 2(5) - 3 = 7 =$ RHS.

Transposing: moving a term from one side to the other changes its sign. In $x + 5 = 12$, transposing 5 gives $x = 12 - 5 = 7$.

## 2.3 Some Applications
Example 2: The sum of two numbers is 74. One of the numbers is 10 more than the other. What are the numbers?
Let the smaller number be $x$. The other number is $x + 10$. So $x + (x + 10) = 74$, which gives $2x + 10 = 74$, so $2x = 64$ and $x = 32$. The numbers are 32 and 42.

Example 3: Sanya's mother is three times as old as Sanya. In 12 years the sum of their ages will be 72. Let Sanya's age be $x$; her mother's age is $3x$. Then $(x + 12) + (3x + 12) = 72$, so $4x + 24 = 72$, $4x = 48$, $x = 12$. Sanya is 12 and her mother is 36.

## 2.4 Solving Equations with the Variable on Both Sides
In $2x - 3 = x + 2$ the variable appears on both sides. Transpose $x$ to the LHS and $-3$ to the RHS: $2x - x = 2 + 3$, so $x = 5$.

Example 4: Solve $5x + \frac{7}{2} = \frac{3}{2}x - 14$.
Multiply both sides by 2 to clear fractions: $10x + 7 = 3x - 28$. Then $7x = -35$, so $x = -5$.

## 2.5 Reducing Equations to Simpler Form
Example 5: Solve $\frac{6x + 1}{3} + 1 = \frac{x - 3}{6}$.
Multiply both sides by 6 (the LCM of the denominators): $2(6x + 1) + 6 = x - 3$. So $12x + 2 + 6 = x - 3$, giving $11x = -11$ and $x = -1$.

Common mistakes:
- Forgetting to multiply every term by the LCM, including whole numbers.
- Changing the sign of only one term inside brackets after transposing.
- Dividing by a variable that could be zero.

## 2.6 Equations Reducible to the Linear Form
Some equations look non-linear but become linear after cross-multiplying. For $\frac{x + 1}{2x + 3} = \frac{3}{8}$, cross-multiplying gives $8(x + 1) = 3(2x + 3)$, so $8x + 8 = 6x + 9$, $2x = 1$, and $x = \frac{1}{2}$.

Summary
1. An equation is a statement of equality between two expressions containing a variable.
2. A linear equation in one variable has the variable with highest power 1.
3. We can transpose terms, changing their sign, to solve equations.
4. Equations with fractions are simplified by multiplying both sides by the LCM of the denominators.
