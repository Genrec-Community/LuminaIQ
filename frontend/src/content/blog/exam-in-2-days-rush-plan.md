The exam is in two days. Maybe three. You meant to start earlier, and now it feels like there's no way to cover it all.

Here's the good news: two or three days is a lot more time than one night. With a clear plan, you can cover the topics that carry most of the marks, practise them properly, and still get the sleep that makes it all stick.

## Rule one: you won't cover everything, and that's the plan

In a rush, the biggest mistake is trying to read the whole syllabus once. You'll end up with a thin layer of everything and a solid grip on nothing.

Instead, you're going to pick the topics that matter most, learn those well, and consciously let the rest go. That's not giving up. That's strategy.

## Day 1 morning: triage (1 to 2 hours)

Before you study a single page, figure out [what to study](/blog/what-to-study-for-exam/):

1. Get the syllabus or topic list.
2. Look at two or three past papers. Note which topics come up most and carry the most marks.
3. Mark each topic: **green** (I mostly know this), **amber** (I've seen it, it's shaky), **red** (no idea).
4. Rank the topics by marks.

Your focus list is the high-mark topics that are amber or red. Green topics get a quick review later. Low-mark red topics may get dropped.

## Day 1: learn the big topics

Work through your focus list in blocks of about 45 minutes, with a 10-minute break after each.

For each topic:

- Read or watch just enough to understand it. Summaries and chapter overviews are your friend.
- Close the book and write down what you remember.
- Answer three to five questions on it.
- Make a few flashcards for anything you got wrong.

Aim for four to six blocks. Stop at a reasonable time and sleep for seven to eight hours. Sleep is when your brain stores what you learned today, so it's part of the plan, not a break from it.

## Day 2 morning: review yesterday first

Start by testing yourself on Day 1's topics *before* you look at your notes. This first review is the one that makes yesterday's work last. Fix anything you've forgotten, then carry on with the next topics on your list.

## Day 2 afternoon: past papers

Do at least one past paper, or a big chunk of one, under timed conditions. Then mark it honestly.

This shows you three things: what you know, how the questions are actually phrased, and how fast you need to write. Spend the rest of the afternoon fixing the gaps the paper revealed.

## Day 2 evening (or Day 3): consolidate

- Make your one-page [last minute revision](/blog/last-minute-revision-tips/) sheet.
- Run through your flashcards, wrong ones first.
- Review green topics quickly.
- Do one more timed question.
- Sleep.

If you have a third day, use it for another past paper and a second round of reviews. Don't add lots of new topics on the final day.

## A rush plan at a glance

| When | Focus |
|---|---|
| Day 1 morning | Triage: syllabus, past papers, rank topics |
| Day 1 | Learn high-mark amber and red topics in 45-minute blocks |
| Day 2 morning | Test yourself on Day 1, then new topics |
| Day 2 afternoon | Timed past paper, fix gaps |
| Day 2 evening | One-page sheet, flashcards, sleep |
| Exam morning | Read the sheet once, stay calm |

## What to avoid in a rush

- **All-nighters.** They feel heroic, but they cost focus and memory on exam day.
- **Perfect notes.** Scrappy notes you test yourself on beat pretty notes you never use.
- **Switching subjects constantly.** Finish a topic before you start the next.
- **Doom-scrolling breaks.** Ten minutes on your phone easily turns into forty.

## When it's one week, not two days

If you have a week, use the same method but slower: two or three topics a day, a review of earlier topics every morning, and a past paper every other day. Our full guide on [making an exam study plan](/blog/exam-study-plan/) shows how to lay it out.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pass an exam with 2 days of study?

Often, yes, especially if you focus on high-mark topics and practise with past papers. Two focused days with proper sleep can cover a lot more than people expect.

### How many hours should I study in a day for a rush exam?

Around six to eight focused hours, in 45-minute blocks with breaks, is realistic. More than that usually means lower-quality hours and less sleep.

### Should I skip some topics if I'm short on time?

Yes. Choose based on marks and how often they appear in past papers. It's better to know 70 percent of the syllabus well than 100 percent badly.

### Is it better to study all night or sleep before the exam?

Sleep. Memory consolidation happens during sleep, and being well rested helps focus and recall in the exam hall.
