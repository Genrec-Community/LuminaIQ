It's late, the exam is tomorrow, and you haven't done as much as you meant to. Most of us have been here at least once. Some of us have been here every semester.

Let's not waste time on guilt. Tonight is not the night to fix your study habits. Tonight is about getting the most marks you can out of the hours left. Here's how.

## First, decide how much time you really have

Work backwards from the exam. If it's at 9am, you need to be up by 7, which means sleeping by around midnight or 1am at the latest. That's your hard stop.

Yes, sleep. We'll come back to why, but the short version is that a tired brain in an exam hall throws away marks on things you actually knew. An all-nighter usually costs more than it buys.

Say you've got four hours. That's plenty to make a real difference if you use them on the right things.

## Spend 15 minutes figuring out what matters

Don't open the textbook at page one. Instead, grab whatever tells you what the exam looks like:

- Past papers, if you have them
- The syllabus or topic list
- Anything the teacher said would "definitely come up"
- Chapter summaries

Make a quick list of the topics that show up most and carry the most marks. That's your plan for the night. Anything not on the list is something you're consciously choosing to let go. That's fine. Trying to cover everything thinly is how people end up knowing nothing well.

## Learn the stuff that's quick to learn

Some things give you a lot of marks for very little time:

- **Definitions.** Often asked word for word.
- **Formulas.** Write them out on one page.
- **Diagrams.** Labelled diagrams get easy marks in biology, geography, physics and more.
- **Key dates, names, and lists.** "Give three causes of..." questions.

Make one sheet with all of these. Read it, cover it, try to write it out from memory, check, repeat. Three rounds of that will lock in more than an hour of reading.

## Test, don't reread

This is the most important tip on the page. When you're tired and stressed, rereading notes feels safe. It isn't helping much. The words feel familiar because you're looking at them, but you won't have them in front of you tomorrow.

Instead, after you go through a topic, close everything and answer a question about it. Use past paper questions if you can. If not, ask yourself: *if I had to explain this to someone, what would I say?* Say it out loud. Where you stumble is what you need to look at again.

This is called [active recall](/blog/active-recall-study-method/) and it's far more effective than rereading, especially when time is short.

## Do at least one timed question

If there's a long-answer section, do one under exam conditions. Set a timer, write the answer, then check it against the marking scheme or your notes. You'll learn how much you can write in the time, which topics you can actually explain, and where you lose marks. That's worth a lot of reading.

## What to skip tonight

- **Making beautiful notes.** No time. Scrappy is fine.
- **Brand-new hard topics.** If it's a concept you've never seen and it's complicated, you're unlikely to learn it well tonight. Use the time on things you half-know instead. Turning "sort of" into "yes" is where the marks are.
- **Group chats.** Everyone panicking together helps nobody. Mute it.
- **Energy drinks at 1am.** You'll be wired when you should be sleeping, and wrecked when you should be sharp.

## Then sleep

Here's why this matters. While you sleep, your brain replays and stores what you learned during the day. It's part of how short-term learning becomes something you can actually recall. Skip sleep and a lot of tonight's work doesn't get saved properly.

Sleep also affects focus, reading speed and how calm you feel. Exams punish all of those.

So stop at your hard stop. Put your one-page summary next to your bed.

## In the morning

Read through your one-page sheet once over breakfast. Try to recall the key points without looking. Don't start new material. Get there early, breathe, and remember that the people around you are just as nervous.

## Quick checklist for tonight

1. Set your sleep time and stick to it.
2. List the high-mark topics from past papers.
3. Make one sheet of definitions, formulas and diagrams.
4. Test yourself on each topic instead of rereading.
5. Do one timed question.
6. Sleep.

Good luck. You've got more time than it feels like.
