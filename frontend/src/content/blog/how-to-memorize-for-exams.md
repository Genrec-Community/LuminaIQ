Some exams are about understanding. Many still need you to remember a lot: definitions, formulas, dates, diagrams, vocabulary, long answers. If memorising feels slow and frustrating, you're probably using the wrong method.

These eight techniques are the ones that work best, from everyday basics to tricks for stubborn facts.

## 1. Understand before you memorize

It's much easier to remember something that makes sense. Before memorising a definition, make sure you can explain it in your own words. Before memorising a formula, know what each part means. Memorising without understanding is slow and fragile.

## 2. Recall, don't reread

The most important rule. Reading something ten times is far less effective than reading it once and then trying to recall it several times. Cover it, say it or write it, check, repeat.

Flashcards are the classic tool: question on one side, answer on the other. Say the answer before you flip. Learn more in our guide to [active recall](/blog/active-recall-study-method/).

## 3. Space it out

Memorising something five times in one evening works for tomorrow. Memorising it once today, once in two days, and once next week works for months. Same effort, spread out, much better result. That's the [spacing effect](/blog/spaced-repetition-schedule/).

## 4. Chunk long information

Your memory handles small groups better than long lists. A phone number is easier as 98765 43210 than as ten separate digits. Do the same with study material: group a list of twelve causes into three groups of four, or split a long answer into three key points.

## 5. Use mnemonics for stubborn lists

When you just need to remember an order or a list, a mnemonic helps. The first letters of each item make a word or a silly sentence. The sillier, the more memorable. Make your own; they stick better than borrowed ones.

## 6. Picture it

Turn facts into images. Imagine the process happening, or place items around a room you know well and "walk" through it in your head (the memory palace technique). Images and places are surprisingly easy to remember.

## 7. Write it and say it

Using more than one sense helps. Write a definition out from memory, then say it out loud. For diagrams, draw them from memory and label them. For formulas, write them and explain what they mean.

## 8. Sleep on it

Sleep isn't wasted study time. While you sleep, your brain strengthens what you learned during the day. A short review before bed, then a quick recall test in the morning, is one of the best memory combinations there is.

## How to memorize different things

| What | Best technique |
|---|---|
| Definitions | Understand, then flashcards and recall out loud |
| Formulas | Know what each term means, write from memory daily |
| Dates and names | Flashcards, timelines, link to events |
| Diagrams | Draw and label from memory, check, repeat |
| Long answers | Learn the 3 to 5 key points, then expand each |
| Vocabulary | Daily flashcards, use words in sentences |

## A 7-day memorization plan

- **Day 1:** Understand the material, make flashcards, first recall round.
- **Day 2:** Recall everything, focus on what you missed.
- **Day 4:** Recall again, faster this time.
- **Day 7:** Final recall round. Anything you still miss gets daily reviews until the exam.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the fastest way to memorize for an exam?

Understand the material first, then test yourself with flashcards or by writing it from memory, several times spread over a few days. Recall beats rereading by a wide margin.

### How do I memorize long answers?

Break the answer into three to five key points and memorise those first. Then practise expanding each point into a few sentences from memory.

### How can I remember what I study for longer?

Review it on a spaced schedule, for example after one day, three days and a week, and test yourself each time instead of rereading. Good sleep helps consolidate memories.

### Do mnemonics really work?

Yes, especially for lists and sequences. Mnemonics you create yourself tend to be the most memorable. They're best combined with understanding, not used instead of it.
