You know you should study. You've known for days. And yet here you are, reading an article about studying instead of studying. (No judgement. We wrote it, after all.)

Getting started is often the hardest part. Once you're ten minutes in, it usually gets easier. So this post is about one thing: getting you to those first ten minutes, right now.

## Why starting is so hard

Procrastination usually isn't laziness. It's avoiding a bad feeling. The task feels huge, or confusing, or boring, or you're worried you'll do it badly. Putting it off makes the feeling go away for a while, so your brain keeps choosing that.

The way out is to make the first step so small that the bad feeling doesn't kick in.

## Make the first step tiny

Don't aim to "study chemistry". Aim for something you can finish in five minutes:

- Read the summary at the end of one chapter.
- Answer three questions from a past paper.
- Write out one formula from memory.
- Watch one short explanation of one concept.

That's it. Promise yourself you can stop after that if you want. Most of the time, you won't want to. And if you do stop, you still did something, which is more than you'd done before.

## Set a timer for 10 minutes

Tell yourself you only have to work for 10 minutes. Set a timer and start. When it rings, you're allowed to stop.

This works because 10 minutes feels manageable, and once you're in the middle of something, continuing is easier than starting. If you want to keep going, set the timer for 25 and see where you get. (There's more on timed sessions in [how to focus while studying](/blog/how-to-focus-while-studying/).)

## Start with a question, not a page

Reading feels like a slog. Questions feel like a puzzle. If you're stuck, skip the reading and try a question first. Even if you can't answer it, now you have a reason to look something up, and looking things up with a purpose is much more engaging than reading from the top.

This also happens to be one of the most effective ways to learn. Trying to answer before you're ready makes the answer stick better when you find it. It's a version of [active recall](/blog/active-recall-study-method/).

## Remove one obstacle

Look at what's stopping you, and fix just one thing:

- Can't find your notes? Find them, and that's step one done.
- Desk is a mess? Clear a space just big enough for a book.
- Phone keeps pulling you away? Put it in another room for 10 minutes.
- Don't know where to start? Pick the topic that comes up most in past papers.

Each obstacle you remove makes starting easier next time too.

## Lower the bar on "good enough"

Sometimes we don't start because we want to do it properly: perfect notes, a full afternoon, the right music. Then the right moment never comes.

Today, aim for scrappy. Scribbled notes are fine. Half an hour is fine. Getting a question wrong is fine. Studying badly beats not studying at all, and it often turns into studying well.

## Then do it again tomorrow

The goal isn't one heroic study session. It's showing up most days. Ten minutes a day, every day, adds up faster than a big session once a week that leaves you exhausted and avoiding the books again.
