Most students are never taught *how* to study. We're told to "revise" and left to figure it out, which usually means rereading notes and highlighting until the page glows.

Those habits feel productive, but research on learning keeps finding the same thing: they're among the least effective ways to study. The good news is that the methods that do work are simple. Here's a five-step method you can use for any subject.

## The five-step study method

### 1. Preview (5 minutes)

Before you read a chapter, skim it. Read the headings, the summary, the diagrams and the end-of-chapter questions. You're building a mental map so the details have somewhere to go.

### 2. Learn in small chunks (20 to 30 minutes)

Read one section, not the whole chapter. Focus on understanding, not memorising. If something doesn't make sense, stop and fix it now, by asking a teacher, rereading the earlier section or looking up a simpler explanation.

### 3. Recall (5 minutes)

Close the book. Write or say everything you remember from that section. Then check. This is [active recall](/blog/active-recall-study-method/), and it's the most powerful habit in this whole guide. Struggling to remember is exactly what makes the memory stronger.

### 4. Practise (15 minutes)

Answer questions on the section: end-of-chapter questions, past papers, or flashcards. For maths and science, do problems, not just reading worked examples. Mix up question types so you learn to recognise *which* method a question needs.

### 5. Review later (a few minutes each)

Come back to the topic after one day, a few days, and two weeks. Each review is short. Together, they move the topic from "I studied that once" to "I know that". That's [spaced repetition](/blog/spaced-repetition-schedule/).

## Study habits that waste the most time

| Habit | Why it doesn't work well | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Rereading | Familiarity feels like knowing, but isn't | Close the book and recall |
| Highlighting | Marks what's important, doesn't learn it | Turn highlights into questions |
| Copying notes neatly | Slow, mostly passive | Write short notes from memory |
| Cramming | Fades within days | Spread study across days |
| Studying with your phone nearby | Breaks focus every few minutes | Phone in another room |

## How to study different subjects

**Maths and physics.** Understand a worked example, then solve similar problems without looking. Mistakes are where the learning happens, so keep an error log.

**Biology, chemistry and geography.** Lots of terms and processes. Use diagrams, flowcharts and flashcards. Explain each process step by step out loud.

**History and social studies.** Build timelines and cause-and-effect chains. Practise writing short answers that use evidence.

**Languages.** Little and often. Daily flashcards for vocabulary, and speaking or writing short sentences from memory.

**Accounting and economics.** Practise formats and calculations until they're automatic, then focus on explaining *why*.

## Make your environment work for you

- Same place, same time each day, if you can. It becomes a habit.
- Phone out of the room, or at least face down and silent.
- Water nearby, good light, a clear desk.
- Use a timer: 25 to 50 minutes on, 5 to 10 off. If you struggle to start, our guide on [how to start studying](/blog/learn-now-start-studying/) helps.

## Take care of the basics

Sleep, food and movement aren't extras. Sleep is when memories are stored. Regular exercise improves focus. Skipping meals makes concentration harder. When exams are close, these are the first things to protect, not the first to drop.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most effective way to study?

Active recall (testing yourself instead of rereading) combined with spaced repetition (reviewing over several days) is the most consistently effective approach across subjects.

### How can I study smarter, not harder?

Spend less time rereading and highlighting and more time answering questions from memory. Prioritise high-mark topics and review them on a schedule instead of cramming.

### How long should I study at a time?

Most students focus best in blocks of 25 to 50 minutes with 5 to 10 minute breaks. After three or four blocks, take a longer break.

### Do I need to make notes to study effectively?

Short notes help, especially if you write them from memory. Long, neat notes copied from the textbook take a lot of time and teach relatively little.
